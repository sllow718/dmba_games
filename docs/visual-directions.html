Basket Detective · visual design review

Three directions for the game's look

All three show the same moment so they can be compared directly. The moment is Mission 3, question 2: "What is the confidence of Bread → Butter?" The student has answered 50% (wrong), and a hint is showing.

Why the current mockups look generated

  • Everything is a bordered, rounded box. Panels, cards, chips, metrics and receipts all get the same treatment, so nothing stands out.
  • A dark slate panel with one yellow accent. This is the default "AI dashboard" look and doesn't come from the subject.
  • Small uppercase monospace labels everywhere. "MISSION 3 · CHECKPOINT" on every block adds noise without adding meaning.
  • Ornaments instead of information. Pills, ★★☆ stars and emoji locks fill space but don't explain anything.
  • Items have no visual identity. Bread and Milk look the same, so students can't see a pattern. Seeing patterns is the whole point of association rules.
  • The receipts are decoration. The jagged paper edge is cute, but it hides the structure the course teaches: transactions × items.

A calm, printed-worksheet feel. The question gets the space and the hint reads like a tutor's note in the margin.

Type

Newsreader (serif) for questions and hints. Public Sans for the interface.

Colour

White paper, black ink, a red pen for corrections and a yellow highlighter for data.

Signature detail

Highlighter strokes on the data and a red-pen strike-through on wrong answers. Students already know these marks from paper.

Fits / risks

Feels serious and readable for university students. The risk is that it feels like homework rather than a game.

Type

Archivo throughout, using its condensed widths for large numbers and labels. Tabular figures in all data.

Colour

Cool neutral greys and black ink. One fixed colour per item, kept the same in every mission.

Signature detail

The fraction under the question fills in as the student works. The hint fades rows that don't count, so the denominator is visible.

Fits / risks

Teaches the data structure itself, and the same matrix carries Apriori in Mission 5. Every column is labelled with its item name, so it doesn't rely on colour alone.

3 · Shop counter

Tactile and game-like. Receipts are paper slips on the counter, items are coloured tokens, and the shop owner talks to the student.

Recommendation

Go with 2 · Data matrix and borrow the margin-note hint style from the Workbook. The matrix makes the concept itself visible: support is how full a column is, confidence is a fraction of the highlighted rows. The look also carries over to the later activities, which all work on tables of data.
