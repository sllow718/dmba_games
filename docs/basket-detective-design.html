DMBA Games · Activity 01 · Design proposal v0.1

Basket Detective

Students work as the data analyst for a small neighbourhood grocery, Corner Mart. They read till receipts, find which products are bought together, and learn to tell a useful rule from a misleading one. Along the way they compute support, confidence and lift, and use the Apriori principle to skip work.

Audience: intro data-mining / business analytics studentsSession: 20–30 minPlatform: browser, desktop + tablet + phone

What students should walk away with

Each mission targets one idea. A short review explains it first, in plain words and with one worked example on the receipts. The student then applies it straight away on the board and in the checkpoint questions.

Transactions & itemsets

A receipt is a transaction. Any group of items is an itemset.{Bread, Butter}

Support

How common is this combination across all receipts?supp(X) = count(X) / N

Confidence

Of the receipts with X, how many also have Y?conf(X→Y) = supp(X∪Y) / supp(X)

Lift

Does X actually raise the chance of Y, compared with Y on its own?lift = conf(X→Y) / supp(Y)

Apriori principle

If an itemset is rare, every bigger set containing it is rare too, so it can be pruned without counting.supp(X∪Y) ≤ supp(X)

Business judgement

Turn a strong rule into an action: bundle, shelf placement or promotion. Reject rules with lift ≤ 1.

Game structure

A hub page lists every activity. Activity 01 is a run of six missions that unlock in order. Each mission follows the same loop:

BriefThe store owner sets the problem.
Quick review1–2 minutes: what the idea is, how to calculate it, one worked example.
ExploreStudents work with the receipts on the interactive board.
Checkpoint3–5 questions on what the mission just taught.
Wrong? Hint + retryA hint appears and the student tries again, as many times as needed.
ScoreStars depend on how many answers were right first time.
  1. 1

    Read the Receipts

    Sort 10 till receipts. Tap every receipt that contains Bread. Count them. The game names what they just did: that is the support count.

    teaches: transactions, itemsets, support count
  2. 2

    Support Hunt

    The owner only cares about combinations bought in at least 30% of baskets. Students drag a minimum-support slider and predict which itemsets survive before the game reveals them.

    teaches: support as a percentage, min-support threshold
  3. 3

    If This, Then That

    Build a rule by picking items for the "if" side and the "then" side. Receipts light up as they match. Students type the confidence before the meter reveals it.

    teaches: rules X → Y, confidence, direction matters (Bread→Butter ≠ Butter→Bread)
  4. 4

    The Lift Lens

    The owner's nephew claims "half of bread buyers buy milk, put them together!" Students compare that 50% with how often anyone buys milk (60%) and decide whether the rule is real.

    teaches: lift, baseline, why high confidence can mislead
  5. 5

    Apriori Climb

    Climb an itemset lattice level by level. Students cross out rare itemsets, and the game rewards them for pruning bigger sets without counting them. A counter tracks "receipts scanned" so efficiency is visible.

    teaches: Apriori principle, candidate generation, pruning
  6. 6

    Manager's Meeting

    The final challenge uses a bigger 30-receipt dataset. Students find the top three rules that pass support ≥ 20%, confidence ≥ 60% and lift > 1, then pick a store action for each and defend it in one sentence.

    teaches: the full pipeline, interpretation, business recommendation

Quick review before each mission

Before a mission's activity starts, the student goes through a short review of what the mission teaches. It is 3–4 cards and takes 1–2 minutes. Every review uses the same four cards, so students always know where to look:

Card 1

What is it?

The idea in one or two plain sentences, tied to the store: "how common is this combination?"

Card 2

How to calculate it

The formula, written in words first and then in symbols.

Card 3

Worked example

One example on the real receipts, revealed step by step. Matching receipts light up as each step appears.

Card 4

Remember

2–3 key points and common mistakes, then the Start mission button.

The review is required the first time and skippable on replays. A Review button on every mission and checkpoint screen reopens it at any point, and the step-3 hints link back to the worked example. After the mission, the cards are saved to the student's formula-card glossary.

Questions, hints and retries

Every mission ends with a checkpoint of 3–5 questions on what that mission taught. A student cannot leave the checkpoint until every question is answered correctly, so every student finishes having got each answer right at least once. When an answer is wrong, a hint appears and the student tries again. They can retry as many times as they need.

Hints get more specific with each wrong attempt on the same question:

1st wrong attempt

Targeted nudge

Aimed at the mistake behind the answer they picked. Each wrong option is written for a common misconception and has its own hint.

You counted every receipt. Confidence only looks at receipts that contain the "if" item.
2nd wrong attempt

Point to the data

Highlights the receipts that matter on the board and gives the first number.

6 receipts have Bread (now highlighted). How many of those also have Butter?
3rd wrong attempt onwards

Worked step

Shows the calculation with one step left for the student to finish. The game never gives the answer outright.

5 of the 6 Bread receipts have Butter. Confidence = 5 ÷ 6 = ?
Correct

Explain why

Every correct answer comes with a one-line explanation, so a lucky guess still teaches something.

Right: 83% of Bread buyers also bought Butter.

Scoring with retries

Retries are unlimited, so stars measure how quickly the student got there. A question answered right on the first try earns 3 points, on the second try 2 points, and later 1 point. A mission's stars come from its share of the maximum points: ≥ 90% ★★★ · ≥ 60% ★★☆ · otherwise ★☆☆. Replaying a mission keeps the best score. The game also logs which questions needed the most attempts, ready for a teacher view later.

The starter dataset

Ten receipts, small enough to count by hand and built so every key lesson appears in them: a strong rule, a direction-dependent rule, a rule with lift below 1, a rule with lift exactly 1, and an itemset the Apriori principle prunes.

Proposed build

A static web app, so it can be hosted for free on GitHub Pages or Vercel straight from this repository.

Stack

  • Vite + React + TypeScript
  • Plain CSS with design tokens
  • Vitest for the rule engine

Shared core

  • Hub, routing, player profile
  • Scoring, stars, XP, badges
  • Progress store (localStorage)

Activity module

  • activities/association-rules/
  • engine.ts: support, confidence, lift, Apriori
  • missions/*.json: questions, options, hint ladders
  • datasets/*.json

Milestones

  • M1: engine + tests, Missions 1–3
  • M2: Missions 4–6, scoring, hub
  • M3: sandbox, extra datasets, polish

Decisions I need from you

  1. Who are the students?The design assumes university or polytechnic students in an intro data-mining or business analytics course. Younger students would need simpler wording and fewer formulas.
  2. Does the store theme work for your class?Corner Mart grocery is the default. Alternatives: a bookshop, a bubble-tea shop, or an online store.
  3. Do you need teacher tracking in the first release?Without it the game is a static site with no server. With it we need accounts and a small backend.
  4. Is the React + TypeScript stack fine?A plain HTML/JS version is also possible if you or other teachers want to edit the code without a build step.
