DMBA Games · Activity 01 · Design proposal v0.2

Basket Detective

Students work as the data analyst for Mrs Tan's neighbourhood minimart, Corner Mart. They go through the till receipts, find which products are bought together, and learn to tell a useful rule from a misleading one. Along the way they calculate support, confidence and lift, and use the Apriori principle to skip work.

Audience Polytechnic students, intro data mining / business analyticsSession 20–30 minPlatform browser: laptop, tablet, phoneLook Shop counter

What students should walk away with

Each mission targets one idea. A short review explains it first, in plain words and with one worked example on the receipts. The student then applies it on the board and answers checkpoint questions on it.

Transactions & itemsets

A receipt is a transaction. Any group of items is an itemset.

{Bread, Butter}

Support

How common is this combination across all receipts?

supp(X) = count(X) ÷ N

Confidence

Of the receipts with X, how many also have Y?

conf(X→Y) = count(X∪Y) ÷ count(X)

Lift

Does X actually raise the chance of Y, compared with Y on its own?

lift = conf(X→Y) ÷ supp(Y)

Apriori principle

If an itemset is rare, every bigger set that contains it is rare too, so it can be skipped without counting.

supp(X∪Y) ≤ supp(X)

Business judgement

Turn a strong rule into an action: a bundle, a shelf placement or a promotion. Reject rules with lift ≤ 1.

How a mission runs

The hub lists every activity. Activity 01 has six missions, and each one is a separate page. A mission unlocks only after the previous one is complete. Every mission follows the same steps:

Brief from Mrs TanQuick reviewExplore the receiptsCheckpoint questionsWrong? Hint, then retryResults & unlock next
  1. Aisle1

    Read the Receipts

    Go through 10 till receipts. Tap every receipt that contains Bread and count them. That count is the support count.

    Teaches: transactions, itemsets, support count
  2. Aisle2

    Support Hunt

    Mrs Tan only cares about combinations bought in at least 30% of baskets. Students drag a minimum-support slider and predict which itemsets survive before the game reveals them.

    Teaches: support as a percentage, the minimum-support threshold
  3. Aisle3

    If This, Then That

    Build a rule by picking items for the "if" side and the "then" side. Receipts light up as they match. Students work out the confidence before the meter shows it.

    Teaches: rules X → Y, confidence, why Bread → Butter ≠ Butter → Bread
  4. Aisle4

    The Lift Lens

    Mrs Tan's nephew says "half of bread buyers buy milk, so put them together!" Students compare that 50% with how often anyone buys milk (60%) and decide whether the rule is real.

    Teaches: lift, baseline rate, why high confidence can mislead
  5. Aisle5

    Apriori Climb

    Stock the shelves one level at a time. Rare itemsets get stamped "too rare", and students earn bonus points for skipping bigger sets without counting them. A counter shows how many receipt checks they saved.

    Teaches: Apriori principle, candidate generation, pruning
  6. Aisle6

    Manager's Meeting

    The final challenge uses a bigger dataset of 30 receipts. Students find three rules that pass support ≥ 20%, confidence ≥ 60% and lift > 1, then choose a store action for each.

    Teaches: the full process, interpretation, business recommendation

Questions, hints and retries

Every mission ends with a checkpoint of 3–5 questions. A student can't leave the checkpoint until every question is answered correctly. When an answer is wrong, Mrs Tan gives a hint and the student tries again, as many times as they need. Each wrong attempt on the same question gets a more specific hint:

1st wrong attempt

Targeted nudge

Written for the misconception behind the option they picked.

"50% counts every customer. I only want the ones who bought bread."
2nd wrong attempt

Point to the data

Lights up the receipts that matter and gives the first number.

"6 receipts have bread. I've lit them up. How many also have butter?"
3rd attempt onwards

Worked step

Shows the calculation with one step left to finish. The answer is never given outright.

"5 of the 6 bread receipts have butter. So 5 ÷ 6 is…?"
Correct

Explain why

Every correct answer comes with a one-line explanation, so a lucky guess still teaches something.

"Right. 83% of my bread buyers also take butter."

Scoring with retries

A correct answer earns 3 points on the first try, 2 on the second and 1 after that. Stars come from the share of the maximum points: 90% or more earns three stars, 60% or more earns two, and anything less earns one. Replays keep the best score. The game also records which questions took the most attempts, ready for a teacher view later.

The starter dataset

The starter set has ten receipts, which is small enough to count by hand. It is built so every key lesson appears in it: a strong rule, a rule that depends on direction, a rule with lift below 1, a rule with lift exactly 1, and an itemset that the Apriori principle skips.

Visual style

The game uses the Shop counter direction, adjusted for polytechnic students. The setting is playful, but the language is direct and the data is always readable.

Setting

Sage-green counter, paper receipts and pressable buttons. Each activity is a different shop on the same street.

Items

Every item has a fixed colour and a two-letter tag that never change, so patterns are visible without reading.

Type

Baloo 2 for headings and buttons. Nunito Sans for everything students read.

Mrs Tan

The shop owner gives every brief, hint and verdict. Feedback feels like a conversation, not an error message. She speaks like a business owner, not a teacher.

Kept adult

No mascots, confetti or baby talk. Real business numbers and decisions. Stars and badges are kept small.

Accessible

Colour is always paired with a tag or a symbol (✓ ✕). Text meets AA contrast, everything is keyboard-operable, and tilts and motion are turned off under reduced motion.

Proposed build

A static web app that can be hosted free on GitHub Pages or Vercel straight from this repository.

Stack

Vite + React + TypeScript, plain CSS with design tokens, and Vitest for the rule engine.

Shared core

Hub, routing and page guards for locked missions, scoring, and a progress store.

Activity module

activities/association-rules/ with engine.ts, missions/*.json (questions, hints) and datasets/*.json.

Milestones

M1: engine, tests, hub, map and Missions 1–3. M2: Missions 4–6 and scoring. M3: sandbox, more datasets, polish.

Decisions

  1. Who are the students?Polytechnic students. Wording, numbers and tone have been set for them.
  2. Visual direction?Shop counter.
  3. Does the minimart setting work for your module?Corner Mart is the default. A bubble-tea shop or an online store would also work.
  4. Do you need teacher tracking in the first release?Without it, the game is a static site with no server. With it, we need logins and a small backend.
  5. Is React + TypeScript fine?A plain HTML/JS version is possible if lecturers want to edit the code without a build step.
